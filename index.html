Methodology & Data Sources

How your shrinkage exposure is calculated, and why the numbers are accurate

Every figure in this calculator is derived from published industry data, informed by the founder’s 25 years of operational loss-prevention practice across DACH and Gulf markets. This page explains exactly where each number comes from and what it means for your operation.

Step-by-Step Calculation
1
Baseline Shrinkage Rate
We apply your country and sector-specific shrinkage benchmark as a percentage of net sales. These rates come from the EHI Inventurdifferenzen Report 2025 (DACH), ECR Europe Loss Prevention Report 2024 (Pan-European), and Oliver Wyman GCC Retail Security Index (Gulf). Rates range from 0.42% (Swiss grocery) to 2.8% (UAE electronics), and are updated annually.
Annual Loss = Annual Revenue × Shrinkage Rate
2
Stock Count Overrun (SCO)
Stock count overrun represents the hidden cost of inventory inaccuracy, the time and labour your team spends on recounts, reconciliation, and investigation when stock counts do not match system records. SCO is distinct from shrinkage: it is an operational inefficiency cost, not a theft figure. The default SCO cost per location is €1,200–€4,500 annually depending on operation size and frequency.
SCO Cost = SCO Value per Location × Number of Locations
3
Margin-Adjusted Loss
Shrinkage hits at cost price, but its impact is felt at the margin level. To recover €10,000 of shrinkage loss at a 35% gross margin, a retailer must generate €28,571 of additional sales. The calculator converts your raw loss figure into the equivalent additional revenue you would need to generate to offset it, making the true business impact visible.
Revenue Needed to Offset = Annual Loss ÷ Gross Margin %
4
Recovery Potential
Not all shrinkage is recoverable. The calculator applies a conservative 30% recovery rate as its default. This is a deliberately cautious planning assumption — not a guaranteed or measured result — set towards the lower end of the loss-prevention improvement ranges reported in the published sources cited above (EHI Inventurdifferenzen and ECR Europe Loss Prevention Report) and informed by the founder’s 25 years of operational loss-prevention practice. It reflects what a competently implemented programme can realistically target within its first 12 months, before full maturity; actual recovery depends on your starting position and execution.
Recoverable Loss = Annual Loss × Recovery Rate (30%)
5
Return on Investment
The ROI calculation compares the recoverable loss against a typical professional loss-prevention investment. For illustration, a 10-store fashion retailer with €30M annual turnover might budget €45,000–€95,000 for an annual LP programme (technology, training, audit, consulting) — an indicative planning range, not a quote. Against inputs like these, recoverable shrinkage at the conservative 30% default commonly offsets the investment several times over in the first year. Treat the resulting figure as an illustrative estimate generated from your own inputs, not a forecast of guaranteed return.
ROI = (Recoverable Loss − LP Investment) ÷ LP Investment × 100%

What This Tool Does Not Include, and Why

To keep the output credible and conservative, we deliberately exclude several loss categories that would make the figures significantly larger:

❌ Administrative errors
Pricing errors, receiving discrepancies, and paperwork mistakes typically add 15–25% to total shrinkage. Not included because they require operational audit data to quantify accurately.
❌ Supplier fraud
Short deliveries, invoice manipulation, and collusion with receiving staff. ECR Europe estimates this at 0.1–0.3% of revenue in DACH, excluded from the baseline calculation.
❌ Reputational and insurance costs
Repeat victimisation leads to higher insurance premiums and brand damage in affected locations. Not quantified here but significant for luxury and premium sectors.
❌ Staff replacement costs from theft-related terminations
Average DACH retail staff replacement cost is €8,000–€15,000 per incident including recruitment, training, and productivity loss during the transition period.
The total financial impact of inadequate security in a retail operation is consistently 1.4–1.8× the headline shrinkage figure when all cost categories are included. Our calculator shows you the minimum.
